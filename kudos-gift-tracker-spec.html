The model in one paragraph

Every employee receives $80 of Kudos budget per calendar year. They spend it by sending gift cards to colleagues they want to thank — any amount, any number of people, until the $80 runs out. The budget resets on Dec 31 and does not roll over.

That creates two failure modes HR cares about, and the dashboard is built around both. Budget can go unspent — people forget, or don't know the program exists. And gift cards that were sent can go unclaimed — the money left the company but the recipient never redeemed it. Usage and claim rate are tracked as separate numbers throughout, because the fix for each is different.

The lever HR pulls in both cases is the same: a Nudge, which sends a pre-written Slack message. Nudge buttons appear on every row where a person or team is behind.

HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT
Crimson circles sit on each mockup and are explained in the numbered list directly beneath it. Mockups are rendered at 1240px — scroll them sideways on a narrow screen. All names, teams and figures are fabricated sample data. Mockups keep a light palette in both document themes, because they depict a light product.
§1

Where it lives in the product

Kudos Gift Tracker is a new top-level item in the existing left sidebar, sitting directly under Order history. Like Create order, it is a page with tabbed subpages — here, Dashboard and Network Map. The employee-facing experience in Part 2 is a separate top-level item, My Kudos, visible to everyone rather than to admins.

Tabbed subpages, matching the Digital Gift Card / Digital Voucher pattern on Create order. Network Map is the second tab — detailed in 1.4.

7

Claim tracking — the third requested section, measured on the receiving team. Nudge here goes to people holding unclaimed cards. The dropdown switches the same table between team rows and individual rows.

ASSUMPTION — PACE MARKER
"Behind pace" compares budget used against the share of the calendar year elapsed. This assumes giving should be roughly linear across the year. If Lumen Labs expects seasonal spikes (year-end, review cycles), the comparison baseline needs to change and so does every warning colour on this page.

1.3

What Nudge sends

Nudge sends a pre-written Slack DM. There is no compose step and no editable copy — HR clicks, the message goes. Two templates exist, chosen automatically by which problem the row represents.

Sending rules
Team nudge
Sends Template A as an individual DM to each member below pace. Never posts to a channel.
Rate limit
One nudge per person per 7 days, across both templates. The button greys out in between.
Confirmation
HR sees a toast: "Nudged 6 people in Engineering." No preview dialog — the copy is fixed.
Fallback
If a person has no linked Slack account, the same copy goes by email and the row is flagged.
1

Template A is the message you specified: how much budget is left, and how long until it's gone. The amount and the day count are the only variable fields.

2

Template B covers nudges fired from the claim-tracking section, where the problem is an unredeemed card rather than unspent budget.

3

Sending rules. The rate limit is the important one — without it a single "Nudge everyone" click could DM the same person twice in a morning from two different sections.

ASSUMPTION — TEMPLATE B
You defined the unused-budget message. Template B is my addition, because the claim-tracking section's Nudge button is aimed at people who need to claim a gift, not send one — Template A's copy would be wrong there. Say the word if you'd rather both buttons send the same message.

1

Second tab — the subpage pattern you asked for, mirroring how Create order splits into Digital Gift Card / Digital Voucher / Merchandise.

2

Four controls only. Date range, team filter, what node size encodes, and a cross-team toggle that hides within-team edges — the fastest way to see whether a team talks to anyone outside itself.

3

Node size = kudos received, edge thickness = amount given, edge colour = the giver's team. Nodes settle into team clusters on their own; that clustering is the finding, not a layout choice.

4

A closed loop. Sales thanks Sales and almost nobody else. For HR this is the signal worth chasing — a team that's either siloed or has no cross-functional work.

5

A connector. Leah's edges reach five teams. Dashed ring marks the highest betweenness score — the people holding the org together socially, who rarely show up in any other report.

6

Plain-language read-out. The graph is suggestive; this panel states what it implies in sentences, so an HR user doesn't have to interpret a hairball.

WORTH DECIDING BEFORE BUILD — VISIBILITY
This map makes individual giving and receiving behaviour legible to HR, including who has no connections at all. That is genuinely useful and also genuinely sensitive — in some jurisdictions it is employee relationship data subject to consultation. Recommend: admin-only, aggregate view for managers, and a documented retention window.

§3

Assumptions and open questions

Everything below is a decision I made to keep the mockups coherent, or a question the mockups can't answer. None of it is settled.

A

Budget is per calendar year and doesn't roll over. Every "days remaining" and pace warning in this document depends on that. Monthly or quarterly allocation would change the dashboard's central metric.

B

Pace is linear. "Behind pace" compares spend to the fraction of the year elapsed. If giving is expected to spike at year-end, most of the red on the dashboard is wrong.

C

Template B is my invention. You specified the unused-budget Slack message; the claim-tracking Nudge needed different copy, so I wrote it. Confirm or replace.

D

Currency shown in USD, catalog priced in GBP. The existing product mixes GBP and KRW products under a US$ balance. The $80 allowance is shown in USD throughout; how it converts at purchase needs a real answer.

E

Can you send kudos to anyone, or only outside your own team? The mockups assume anyone. Some programmes block manager-to-report giving to avoid it reading as compensation.

F

Do unclaimed cards return to the sender's budget on expiry? Currently modelled as spent-and-lost, which is why the expiry warning is so prominent. Refunding would soften it.

G

Is the leaderboard visible to employees? Shown here as HR-only. Making it public drives participation but turns recognition into a competition — a real product decision, not a UI one.

H

Network map data sensitivity. Individual-level relationship mapping may require employee notice or works-council consultation in some jurisdictions. Recommend admin-only access and a stated retention window before this ships.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT ISN'T
No engineering design, no data model, no permissions matrix beyond "admin vs. employee", and no mobile layouts. All figures are fabricated. Nothing here is clickable — it's a specification, not a prototype.
